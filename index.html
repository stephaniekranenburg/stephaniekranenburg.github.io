Does Joining the Euro Reduce Inflation? Evidence from a Staggered Difference-in-Differences Design, 1995–2023

Stéphanie Kranenburg · Independent research project · November 2026

Monetary union is often defended as a way to import the credibility of a low-inflation central bank. Using a panel of 28 European countries and the staggered adoption of the euro by 20 of them, this study finds no evidence that joining the euro reduced inflation. A conventional two-way fixed effects estimate even points to higher inflation, but the result is fragile, and an event-study design shows that countries disinflated just before joining to meet the Maastricht criteria.
